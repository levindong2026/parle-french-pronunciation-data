## Try a café order with English or Spanish explanations

The server asks **« Sur place ou à emporter ? »**. Swap your coffee for apple juice, keep the cheese sandwich and take your order to the park. Say both replies in French before opening the explained model and acceptable alternatives.

- [English explanations: try the free café exercise and read the sample](https://getparle.app/books/french-a0-a2/#cafe-order-practice)
- [Spanish explanations: prueba el ejercicio gratuito de cafetería y lee la muestra](https://getparle.app/es/libros/frances-a0-a2/#cafe-order-practice)

The four books work together: learn the situation in the main course, rehearse replies in the speaking book, find clues in graded reading, then recall and reuse vocabulary. Choose the explanation language you read most comfortably. The task and 24-page main-course sample are free; the complete Parle Books collection is a separate paid product. The books include neither recordings nor an App subscription, and this repository's CC BY licence does not apply to them.

## Ask for directions in French: location or instruction?

Read both lines aloud. Which tells you where the station is? Which tells you to turn?

1. **La gare est à droite.**
2. **Tournez à droite.**

Then try a different situation. Ask a passer-by where the museum is. Give a reply that describes its location.

<details>
<summary>Check the answers and one possible museum exchange</summary>

**La gare est à droite.** means “The station is on the right.” **Tournez à droite.** means “Turn right.”

In the new situation, you could ask **« Excusez-moi, où est le musée ? »** and reply **« Le musée est à gauche. »** The museum is on the left.

</details>

- [Practise with a street map and English explanations](https://getparle.app/french-speaking-course/town-directions/#town-map-practice)
- [Listen to the station exchange and check the answers in Spanish](https://getparle.app/es/libros/practica-direcciones-frances/#escena-station)

## Scope and interpretation

The sound chart contains the IPA symbol, common spelling patterns, examples, and a practical mouth or articulation cue used in Parle's learning inventory. French phoneme inventories can differ across linguistic analyses and French varieties; this dataset is a bounded educational inventory, not the only possible analysis of French phonology.

The interactive vowel trainer is a first-party practice surface for 14 oral and nasal vowel entries from the sound chart. It plays checked-in reference audio and does not record, upload, analyse, or score a learner's voice.

The course map contains stage, unit, mission, learner outcome, context, estimated duration, and related-sound metadata. It does not publish full dialogues, answer frames, user recordings, or personal data.

Parle's Phrase Match reports which words in a model phrase speech recognition detected. It is not an accent, pronunciation-quality, fluency, proficiency, or CEFR score. Parle is educational speaking practice, not CEFR certification or speech therapy, and does not promise a native accent.
